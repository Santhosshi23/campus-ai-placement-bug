# Growth Plan — CampusAI: The Placement Bug
### NxtWave Growth Intern Challenge — "Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes"

**Target:** 500 final-year engineering student registrations
**Budget:** ₹2,000 · **Duration:** 7 days · **This is a simulation** — no real outreach was executed.

---

## 1. Who we're targeting & why they care

**Target:** Final-year engineering students (CSE/IT/ECE/EEE/Mech/AI-ML), 2-4 months from placements.

| They care because... | It shows up as... |
|---|---|
| Placement prep is top of mind | Any edge in interviews gets attention |
| AI/GenAI is the #1 resume gap right now | They actively look for "AI project" experience |
| They want something to *show*, not another lecture | Prefer demo-able, practical outcomes |
| Peer & college competition is a strong motivator | They act to protect campus/friend-group reputation |

**What makes them register:** a personalised diagnosis (not generic marketing copy), a 60-second interactive
hook instead of a form, visible campus-vs-campus competition, referral rewards with social currency, and
social proof ("327 students already patched their placement system").

## 2. The campaign plan — prioritised, not exhaustive

Rather than listing 15-20 channels, the plan concentrates ₹2,000 and 7 days on **5 channels** that compound
into each other — seeding → referral loop → daily reinforcement → credibility → awareness.

| # | Channel | What we'd do | Why it works | Est. registrations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **College clubs & core committees** | Share the CampusAI link with 10-15 club leads across 8-10 colleges; ask them to post in official WhatsApp/Telegram groups | Clubs already have trusted, high-reach broadcast lists (100-300 students/college) | **180** |
| 2 | **Student referral loop (in-app)** | Every registrant gets a referral code + 1-tap WhatsApp share; badges/leaderboard nudge them to invite 2-3 friends | Peer invites convert far better than cold outreach, and it compounds for free | **170** |
| 3 | **WhatsApp student communities** | Daily 1-line FOMO updates ("VIT just crossed 120 — is your college next?") in placement/branch/hostel groups | Zero cost, highest trust channel for this audience | **90** |
| 4 | **Email (placement cells)** | One templated email to 5-6 placement cell contacts with the link + one-pager | Borrowed credibility, zero cost | **40** |
| 5 | **Organic social sharing** | Shareable "AI Diagnosis" result card students post to Instagram/LinkedIn | Social currency — students like showing off scores/badges | **20** |
| | **Total (planning estimate)** | | | **500+** |

*All numbers above are planning assumptions for this simulation, not guaranteed outcomes — shown live and
recomputed from real (demo) registration data inside the app's `/admin` Growth Command Center.*

### How the loop actually produces 500
```
Discover (game) → Play → Diagnose → Register → Get referral code
   → Invite friends → College competition → Leaderboard → More registrations
```
Channel 1 seeds the first wave. Every registrant who completes it becomes a referral node for channel 2,
which is the real multiplier — referred users in the live demo data convert at a meaningfully higher rate
than cold/direct traffic (see `/admin → AI Growth Insights`).

## 6. Why the virality loop works (the psychology behind it)

The product is deliberately built as an emotional arc, not a single "share this" button — each step hands
off excitement to the next so a registrant becomes free distribution instead of a dead end.

| Step | What it does | Why it makes someone share |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook, don't pitch** (Hero → mini-game) | No form up front — a terminal "your placement journey has a bug" reveal and `[ START DEBUGGING ]` | Curiosity gap + zero commitment gets a click before anyone has "decided to attend a workshop" |
| **Make it personal** (Diagnosis) | 3-5 questions → one of 5 named profiles + an AI Readiness Score /100, not a generic "thanks" screen | People react to results about *themselves* — the same hook that makes quiz/Wrapped-style content spread |
| **Give them something to show off** (AI Score Card) | A Spotify-Wrapped-style downloadable/shareable image of their score, profile badge and college | A plain "I registered" message isn't shareable; a personal visual result is — it's worth posting on its own, independent of the workshop |
| **Reframe the ask as a dare** (Challenge copy) | Share text says *"Think you can beat my score? 👀"* instead of *"please join this workshop"* | A challenge compels action from the friend; an invite is easy to ignore |
| **Make the stakes bigger than one person** (CampusAI League) | Every registration adds points to the user's *college* on a live inter-college leaderboard | Converts an individual favor into group identity/pride — a much stronger, less awkward reason to post in college groups |
| **Reward the behaviour visibly** (Badges + "Your Squad") | Badge tiers (Bug Hunter → ... → CampusAI Legend) plus a named list of friends actually recruited | Short-term recognition for inviting, and visible proof their influence is real, not an abstract counter |
| **Close the loop with FOMO** (Milestones) | Live counters like "327/500 registered" and "X referrals to next badge" | Scarcity + visible progress bring people back to push one more friend over the line |

No single lever here is novel — personalization, competition, badges, shareable cards and social proof are
standard growth mechanics. The loop works because each one *feeds* the next: a personal result becomes
shareable → sharing is framed competitively → competition creates college pride → pride is rewarded with
visible status → status motivates the next invite → repeat.
